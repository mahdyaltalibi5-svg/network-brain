The Journal

Care for it,
and it lasts.

A well-made suit is built to last for years. Most of the damage comes from a handful of habits. Here is how to avoid them.

Book a Free ConsultationAll articles

By Summit Customs, Salt Lake City · October 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Rest it between wears

Wool recovers its shape when it rests. Give a suit at least a day off between wears; this is the single best reason to own more than one. Empty the pockets every time you take it off.

Hang it properly

Use a broad, shaped wooden hanger that fills the shoulder of the jacket, and hang trousers by the hem or folded over a padded bar. Wire hangers distort the shoulder within weeks. Leave space in the wardrobe so the cloth can breathe.

Brush, don't wash

A soft clothes brush removes dust and the grit that wears down fibres. Brush downward after each wear. Spot-clean small marks with a damp cloth.

Dry clean rarely

Dry cleaning chemicals and the pressing that follows are hard on cloth and canvas. Clean only for a stain or a noticeable smell, a few times a year at most for a suit in regular rotation. Between cleanings, steam to release wrinkles and freshen the cloth.

Store it for the season

For seasonal storage, use a breathable cotton garment bag, never plastic, and add cedar to deter moths. Make sure the suit is clean before storing it; moths are drawn to residue.

Travel with it

Fold the jacket inside out, shoulder into shoulder, and pack it on top. Hang it as soon as you arrive; a steamy bathroom will release most creases. Cloths with natural stretch, like those in our Extender® and Vivo Stretch ranges, recover fastest.

Have it adjusted, not replaced

If your build changes, a suit cut with generous inlays can often be adjusted. Because your pattern stays on file at Summit Customs, a replacement is straightforward when the time comes.

Quick Answers

In short.

How often should I dry clean a suit?

Rarely: only for stains or odours, a few times a year at most. Brush and steam between wears instead.

What hanger is best for a suit?

A broad, shaped wooden hanger that supports the full width of the shoulder.
