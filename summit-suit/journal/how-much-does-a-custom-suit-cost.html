What does a custom suit
actually cost?

The honest answer is that it depends on four decisions. Here is what each one changes, so you can compare quotes properly and know what you are paying for.

Book a Free ConsultationAll articles

By Summit Customs, Salt Lake City · October 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Ask five tailors what a custom suit costs and you will hear five different numbers. That is not evasion. The price of a custom suit is the sum of a handful of choices, and two suits that look alike across a room can be built very differently. Once you know what those choices are, quotes become easy to compare.

1. The cloth

Cloth is usually the single largest variable. Wool is graded by the fineness of its fibre, written as a "Super" number. A Super 110s worsted is hard-wearing and forgiving; Super 150s to 180s is lighter, softer and drapes beautifully, but asks for more care. The mill matters too: a cloth from Dormeuil, Scabal, Loro Piana or Holland & Sherry costs more than an unbranded equivalent because of the yarn, the finishing and the consistency from bolt to bolt.

At Summit Customs you choose from a library of more than 4,000 cloths, including a current seasonal catalog you can browse online. Kobe will tell you plainly when a less expensive cloth is the better choice for how you will actually wear the suit.

2. The construction

Inside the front of every jacket is a layer that gives it shape. In a fused jacket that layer is glued; it is quick to make, but it can stiffen or bubble over time. A half-canvas jacket has a floating canvas through the chest and lapel, so the lapel rolls naturally and the chest moulds to you. A full floating canvas runs the length of the front. It takes the longest to make and is the reason a well-made suit looks better in year five than on day one.

3. How the fit is created

Off-the-rack suits are cut for an average body and then altered. Made-to-measure starts from a pattern adjusted to your measurements and posture. Fully bespoke means a pattern drafted from scratch for one person. Each step takes more skilled time, and time is what you are paying for. Read bespoke vs made-to-measure for the full difference.

4. The details

Linings, working sleeve buttons, a monogram, pick stitching, a waistcoat, a second pair of trousers: each adds work. None of them are required, and a good tailor will help you spend on the details people actually notice.

How Summit Customs prices a suit

Our Collection groups these choices into three tiers so the decision stays simple:

  • The Ascent: a complete made-to-measure suit in Super 110s wool, wool-linen or a durable travel blend. Where most clients start.
  • The Summit: the flagship. Super 120s to 130s, canvassed construction, Bemberg silk lining, and Ermenegildo Zegna cloths on request.
  • The Standard: Super 150s to 180s, full floating canvas, Loro Piana or Scabal on request, and a custom silk lining.

You get an exact price during your complimentary consultation, before you commit to anything. There is no obligation and no upselling: the goal is to understand what you need, not to sell you the most.

How to compare custom suit quotes

  • Ask what the jacket front is made of: fused, half canvas or full canvas.
  • Ask which mill the cloth comes from, and its Super number and weight.
  • Ask how many fittings are included and who does them.
  • Ask whether your pattern is kept for reorders. At Summit Customs it is kept for life, so later orders are faster and need only one fitting.
  • Ask about the timeline. Ours is typically 5 to 8 weeks from consultation to final fitting.

A cheaper suit that needs replacing in two years is not cheaper. Cost per wear is the number that matters.

Quick Answers

In short.

Is a custom suit worth it?

If you wear a suit regularly, yes. A suit cut to your measurements fits better than an altered off-the-rack suit, and canvassed construction lasts years longer, which lowers the cost per wear.
